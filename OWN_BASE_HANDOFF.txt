3. TRAINING HISTORY AND WHAT IT ESTABLISHED

A. Own-base training and GPU infrastructure
Earlier local random-initialization experiments did not deliver useful conversation. The effort moved to CUDA and GCP qualification, then the retained A100-SXM4-40GB run with the 537M architecture. The user allowed scaling up to 1B parameters, but the actual model remains 537M; no parameter expansion occurred merely because more tokens were trained.

Prepared about 10.027B corpus tokens plus separate development data; backed up corpus, tokenizer, source and committed checkpoints to GCS. Qualified restoration and sequential stage execution. A small documented optimizer replay residual means full-trajectory bitwise identity is not claimed.

Base reached 6,000,476,160 tokens on 11 November. At that milestone held-out base cross-entropy was 2.666492, down from 2.695659 at 5.5B and 2.681263 at 5.75B. Historical optimizer throughput was about 103K targets/sec on that A100. These are base-language learning measures, NOT conversation acceptance or current throughput.

B. Why more base training alone was not the immediate fix
Matched 155-update SFT diagnostics at 3B and 6B both scored 0/6 on the original conversation/tool suite. At 6B, SFT used 2,731,938 assistant targets and development loss improved from 1.967975 to 1.469404, without successful tool use in that suite. The controller failed closed and the VM stopped.

Read-only diagnostics found strong teacher-forced tool framing/token predictions but incorrect freely generated actions, routing and arguments. Increasing reply budget from 128 to 512 did not fix the 0/6 result. No large training/serving attention-kernel mismatch or global label/masking failure was established. Teacher-forced loss was a misleading proxy for actual behavior.

Evidence also found weak tool supervision (~3.77% of diagnostic targets), long generic completions mismatched to short evaluation budgets, limited task diversity and noisy synthetic examples. The course correction therefore emphasized verified curriculum, actual generated sessions, termination, tool retention and context use—not endless optimizer comparisons.

C. Repair experiments, with regressions retained in the record
- Initial targeted repair: completion improved 8/35 to 31/35, but exact success only 1/35 to 2/35; a former multi-tool success regressed. Not promoted.
- Broad conversation pilot: 1,000 updates / 18,810,001 assistant targets; development loss improved, but only 1/35 held-out and 0/6 original checks. Not promoted.
- Instruction repairs v1–v3 improved tool routing, decimal arithmetic and structured state tasks, but gains sometimes displaced earlier conversation abilities.
- Conversation repair preserved tools and reached 4/6 original checks and both earlier 28/28 suites. Manual planning quality remained poor despite exact scores.
- Teacher-conversation and constraint-repair branches improved some prose/fact/negation behavior but regressed expanded tool checks to 17/18 or earlier context cases. Not promoted.
- Literal-state repair, step 605: best retained development candidate. It preserves all 18 tool cases and both earlier 28/28 suites; prior conversation-context 27/28, new literal/state 23/24, general exact 4/4, teacher final facts 7/7. These small exposed/development suites do not establish broad conversation quality. Cancelled-task interpretation, sentence constraints and false physical-action claims still failed in manual inspection.
- Latest concise-teacher-repair-v3: 194 updates / 120,540 assistant-target exposures across four passes, not unique tokens. Concise contracts improved 9/32 to 28/32 and context to 28/28, but tools regressed to 17/18 and general exact to 2/4. Rejected; step 605 remains preferred.

Important distinction: SFT assistant-target exposures do not advance the base-training token counter. The model did not complete 10B base tokens.
