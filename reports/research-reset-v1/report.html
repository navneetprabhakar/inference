Own-base training: research and decision

10 November 2026 · Research completed; implementation and training pending

Recommendation: fresh 537M own base, pinned nanochat recipe, approximately 10B tokens, then conversation and broker-specific SFT. Existing authorization remains $50. All larger cost scenarios require approval after qualification.

Decision and confidence

  • Recommend a fresh, randomly initialized nanochat d16 base: 536,871,738 total parameters, width 1024, 2048-token context, 32,768-token vocabulary, approximately 10B retained pretraining targets. Train our own tokenizer. No pretrained weights initialize or replace the student. Preserve the old 124M lineage as a baseline.
  • This is an engineering recommendation, not a published proof that this exact configuration passes our acceptance suite. A guaranteed usable model at a fixed token count and price would be an unjustified promise. The plan makes failures diagnosable and spending bounded.
  • The current pinned d20 is 896,533,746 parameters and about 1.82 times d16 estimated forward/backward FLOPs per token. d24 is 1,384,122,122 and exceeds the user’s 1B ceiling. Counts were measured on the meta device, not inferred from historical model names. Large value-embedding tables mean total parameters alone misrepresent compute.
  • Retire the prior 124M continuation and $400 fixed campaign proposal. Keep their files as history. Do not run more tiny optimizer comparisons or increase layers blindly. Depth changes require a new initialization or a separately validated growth method, and add compute without supplying missing linguistic or instruction data.

What the project evidence actually says

  • The current 124M model has about 247.4M retained training targets. The admitted broad, explanatory and narrative inventories total about 267M tokens; they are not a 10B-token dataset. This is a major scale gap, although insufficient data alone is not a proven complete diagnosis.
  • The A100 diagnostic memorized 128/128 training items but scored 68/128 on unseen entities and 65/128 on changed wording. The latter also changed entities, so it does not isolate paraphrase generalization. Full-pair context performance remained 1–3 of 32 across short LR arms, with severe repetition.
  • CPU/CUDA parity and one-step restart passed for the old implementation. Those are numerical and recovery checks, not conversational competence, and must be repeated for the new stack. The 88k-token/s old 124M microbenchmark cannot forecast d16 at 2048 context.
  • The previous LR comparison covered only roughly 5M tokens per arm at a small batch. It supports neither a fresh-run LR optimum nor the claim that more pretraining cannot help. Equally, falling cross-entropy does not establish useful chat.

Data and training recipe

  • Use a reproducibly shuffled subset of the published ClimbMix mixture, with original provenance and licenses retained. Fetch selected shards, not the full roughly 600GB repack. Split documents before tokenizer fitting; isolate development and final evaluation, remove duplicates and screen benchmark overlap.
  • Acquire enough source material for at least 10B admitted, newly tokenized targets plus packing/filtering margin; plan an initial 20% source allowance and revise from observed yield. Publisher GPT-2 token counts do not equal our new tokenizer counts. Record unique-document exposure, packed positions, discarded tokens and any replay separately. Never silently wrap the dataset to reach the counter.
  • Use the pinned architecture, initialization and Muon/AdamW parameter-group recipe together. Base batch is 524288 tokens, achieved through accumulation on one GPU. Start BF16 with FP32 master parameters; tune only microbatch for memory. Use the upstream width/group scaling rather than transplanting the old uniform 6e-5 LR.
  • Configure the training horizon consistently: 19074 full updates produce 10,000,269,312 targets, explicitly reported as approximately 10B. Set the target-data ratio using the implementation’s scaling parameters (transformer matrices plus output head), not total weights, and log all resolved LRs/decay. Preserve the 40-update warmup, 0.65 linear warmdown fraction and 0.05 terminal LR fraction unless a documented stability failure requires change.
  • One continuous schedule crosses 1B, 3B, 6B and 10B checkpoints; do not restart the schedule at phase boundaries. Use inexpensive fixed development losses approximately every 250M targets and fuller assessments at milestones. Downsize upstream default evaluation volume for a single-GPU budget. Measure evaluation overhead separately.

Required engineering before spending on the main run

  • Pin the repository, resolved package lock, CUDA image, attention kernel binaries, data manifest and tokenizer hash. The project’s private VMs cannot assume public pip or Hugging Face access. Stage a complete offline installation through GCS and verify it on a fresh VM.
  • Implement exact base and SFT restart: model, optimizer, scheduler/step, RNG states, data order, packing buffers, current prefetched batch and cursor. Upstream data resume is approximate and SFT saves only at completion. Verify uninterrupted versus restarted next batches and next optimizer updates; record any nondeterministic numerical tolerance explicitly.
  • Write local atomic checkpoint bundles with hashes, upload all components, then a final COMMITTED manifest. Restore only complete validated bundles. Retain latest two plus selected milestones. Exercise interrupted upload, corrupt checkpoint and VM-loss recovery from GCS, not just same-process reload. Checkpoint at an optimizer boundary roughly every 15 minutes.
  • For SFT, use summed assistant-target loss divided by total valid assistant tokens across accumulation, tested with uneven masks. Upstream equal averaging of microbatch means is a different objective when target counts vary. Mask prompts and tool results. Pack complete conversations or reject/log oversized examples; do not truncate a JSON call or assistant answer silently.
  • Adapt native conversation framing to the existing calculate/convert broker; disable upstream automatic calculator execution. Train and serve identical serialization. Validate call IDs, permission checks, arguments, result grounding and stop tokens. Keep tool validation distinct from model semantic accuracy.
  • Before full training, test tiny-config CPU/CUDA forward and gradient parity, causal masking, padding/packing, cached versus uncached inference, tokenizer round trips, loss masks and exact resume. Demonstrate the new serving path locally with a tiny random model; later benchmark the full checkpoint on the Mac. This is not yet implemented or passed for nanochat.
  • Use provider runtime limits plus an independent cleanup mechanism and ledger-based leases. Budget alerts alone are not a spending stop. Include queue/setup time, persistent disks and GCS charges. Do not renew a lease unless remaining budget protects SFT, evaluation and cleanup.

Conversation, context and tool training

  • After base training, start with filtered smol-smoltalk general conversation and the compatible upstream instruction curriculum. Remove examples that invoke unsupported tools. Measure target tokens and development quality before choosing epochs; do not mechanically repeat a dataset to fill a budget.
  • Add broker-generated lessons verified by actual execution: ordered arithmetic, units, dependent calls, correction, missing operands, no-call decisions, permission denial and error recovery. General conversation must remain represented during this stage. Freeze sampling weights by assistant tokens in the launch manifest after counting the data; a proposed 70% general/30% targeted starting allocation is an experiment, not a proven literature optimum.
  • Build crossed diagnostic cells: familiar/unseen entities × familiar/unseen templates; counterfactual facts; latest correction versus stale fact; distractors; context lengths 256/512/1024/2048; answer versus clarify. Split by template family and source document, not random rows. Evaluate with and without supplied context to distinguish retrieval, grounding and instruction failures.
  • Run one bounded SFT transfer probe at 3B using a separate copy; never merge it back into base. If base metrics improve but instruction transfer is poor, investigate masking, examples and serialization. If all source losses deteriorate, investigate training/data first. Do not spend the rest of the budget on more web text by default.
  • Score raw generation and constrained tool decoding separately. A valid JSON object or blocked unsafe call is not proof of correct tool arguments. Keep repetition penalties as a measured decoding option; they do not repair wrong context. Only trial an unlikelihood objective if residual repetition warrants a matched comparison.
  • Do not begin RL/DPO or parameter growth in this campaign. If 10B plus competent SFT fails, publish the failure by category, preserve the best checkpoint and propose one targeted next experiment. More tokens, better task data or a larger model require evidence and another budget decision.

Acceptance and stop rules

  • Freeze development rubrics and final acceptance before training. Preserve the existing 20-session/62-turn test: at least 16 complete sessions, all 16 expected executions correct, zero incorrect or unauthorized executions. Preserve the original 100-session gate: at least 60 overall, 36/60 prose, 18/30 tool sessions, 24/30 correct first calls and 9/10 no-call sessions, plus zero security violations.
  • Use separate development thresholds for checkpoint selection: at least 80% complete sessions, 98% valid tool frames and 95% correct arguments/result-grounded responses. Do not tune repeatedly against sealed acceptance. If previously viewed test material has contaminated training design, add a genuinely independent final set without weakening the original gates.
  • Calibrate the harness on known-correct scripted fixtures and an optional reference model used only for evaluation. Capture complete transcripts and failure categories; report denominators and confidence intervals. Final delivery requires the actual local model, real broker, load/restart instructions, measured memory/latency and acceptance evidence.
  • Stop immediately for nonfinite training, corrupted data, leakage, broken recovery or insufficient reserved budget. Investigate sustained held-out deterioration using paired documents and uncertainty, rather than reacting to one noisy sample. Base chat failure before instruction training is not by itself a stop rule. A plateau across substantive milestones triggers diagnosis before further spend.

Compute, cost and schedule

  • Preferred benchmark candidate: one H100 80GB Flex-start VM. A100 40GB Flex-start is the fallback, with on-demand A100 only after explicit cost reforecast. A100 80GB currently has zero regional quota in the inspected region; H100 quota was not exposed by the legacy quota response and remains unverified. Availability is not established for any candidate.
  • At the listed rates H100 must exceed A100 40GB Flex throughput by 2.395 times to be cheaper per token. Choose using measured time and dollars per retained target, not GPU branding. Benchmark one candidate first; a second sequential comparison only if the cost decision remains unresolved and the qualification allowance permits it.
  • The qualification phase must include 30–60 minutes of steady-state d16 training, real checkpoint upload, evaluation overhead and a forced restore. Exclude compilation from steady-state throughput but charge it in total cost. Budget qualification at $15, with a $20 absolute subcap only after reconciling the existing $50 project ledger. This report launches nothing.
  • The scenarios below are arithmetic, not measured throughput or a quote. Fixed allowances total $95: prior compute $5, CPU preparation $10, qualification $15, SFT $35, assessment $10, storage/operations/egress $20. Add 25% contingency to the subtotal; this is not a tax calculation. Reprice SFT from actual targets and measured throughput before requesting the campaign cap.
  • Allow roughly 1–2 working days for data and recovery engineering as a planning estimate. Conditional on readiness and capacity, base training spans about 28–69 GPU hours across the illustrative rates, followed by SFT/evaluation. A 3–6 day overall path is plausible only if these assumptions hold; queue time, fixes and data yield can extend it. No firm completion ETA before qualification.
  • The only approved total remains $50. Prior compute estimate is $4.85, not a final invoice; a separate $10 ancillary reserve leaves $35.15 provisionally unallocated. Reconcile storage and billing before any launch. Neither the old $400 proposal nor these scenarios authorize a larger spend.

Current state and next decision

  • No training or GPU provisioning was performed during this review. The prior bounded diagnostics completed and their VM/disks were removed. GCS backups remain. The current model is still not a usable assistant.
  • This is a researched implementation plan, not a completed migration. Next: implement and test the recovery/data/serving changes, then qualify one GPU within the existing cap. Return the measured throughput, concrete data inventory, revised phase costs and pass/fail evidence before requesting a long-run budget.

Primary-source evidence

  1. Training methodology: Use a complete recipe, calibrated evaluations and substantial training horizons. Small ablations do not establish long-run quality. This supports the experimental method, not a guarantee for our model.
  2. A real GPT-2 10B recipe: The 124M recipe uses 1024 positions, 524288-token batches and peak LR 0.0006. Its distributed base-model result does not validate our small-batch continuation or chat quality.
  3. End-to-end training implementation: Reviewed 15 pinned source files. Supplies tokenizer, pretraining, instruction training and inference. Public speedrun uses different hardware, size and precision from this proposal; its advertised cost is not our GCP quote.
  4. Published base mixture: ClimbMix provides a documented large-scale mixture. Using its shuffled sample removes an unsupported custom 70/20/10 mixture from the initial experiment.
  5. Actual source terms: Upstream specifies CC-BY-NC-4.0. Preserve upstream terms and attribution despite the repackaged dataset MIT badge. This plan is for the user’s personal research; commercial rights are not asserted.
  6. Smaller conversation dataset: Approximately 460k conversations provide a general instruction starting point. Filter for complete turns and context fit; count actual assistant targets before pricing SFT.
  7. Small-model training evidence: SmolLM2 demonstrates extensive curated pretraining and post-training. It does not prove that our much smaller token budget reaches its capability.
  8. Tool-data verification: APIGen validates format, execution and semantics. Apply that method to our actual broker. Its fine-tuned pretrained-model results are not evidence that a fresh 10B-token base will match them.
  9. Public tool dataset: 60k function-calling examples are a useful reference, not a direct fit for our two supported tools. Access is gated; this plan does not depend on bypassing or obtaining that access.
  10. In-context learning evidence: Distributional properties can encourage in-context learning. This motivates crossed context tests; it does not establish the cause of our failures.
  11. Repetition objective: Unlikelihood training can reduce repetition. Reserve it for a controlled later comparison if repetition persists after adequate base and conversation training; do not penalize required JSON syntax.
  12. GCP Flex pricing: Checked November 10, 2026: H100 single-GPU VM $4.79/h; A100 40GB $2/h; A100 80GB $2.40/h. Taxes excluded. Page announces November price updates, so verify again at provisioning.
  13. Single H100 provisioning: Small A3 High configurations require Spot or Flex-start. Do not assume a single H100 ordinary on-demand VM is available.
  14. Flex lifecycle: Capacity is not guaranteed; Flex-start has bounded runtime. Stage recovery before provisioning and do not promise immediate GPU availability.
  15. Storage costs: Storage, operations and network charges are separate from VM prices. Keep them in the project ledger and enforce retention.
