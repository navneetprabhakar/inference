Repair execution plan — 12 November 2026

Decision
Test a bounded targeted-SFT repair before broader training. The 6B checkpoint
learns familiar protocol examples but fails held-out instruction/action choices.
Increasing the reply limit from 128 to 512 did not improve the original suite.

1. Preserve the same VM, GPU, immutable 6B ancestor and 6B SFT parent.
   Keep the $100 total cap and absolute shutdown cutoff (1794476714).
2. Measure a frozen 20-session held-out development baseline.
3. Train one finite pass over already admitted targeted-context-v3:
   16,463 training dialogues, 873,656 assistant targets, 243 updates.
   No held-out examples enter training. Preserve checkpoint lineage in GCS.
4. Repeat the identical held-out checks and the original development suite.
   Inspect exact tool choices, arguments, broker outcomes, context retention,
   completion and repetition. Token loss alone cannot qualify the repair.
5. Advance only if held-out passed turns increase, invalid actions do not
   increase and the original suite does not regress. Review transcripts.
   If the pilot fails, use observed failure categories to prepare diverse,
   verified instruction/context/tool examples before another bounded trial.
6. Broader conversation tuning follows a successful repair gate. Continue
   toward 10B base tokens only with evidence supporting the remaining spend.
   Keep the 4K context extension after base completion and conversation tuning.

Pilot limits
SFT: at most 1,800 seconds including a 300-second checkpoint reserve.
Individual quality process: at most 600 seconds.
Whole pilot: at most 3,600 seconds / $2.50 compute upper estimate.
Retain the VM between phases; the original total-budget cutoff still applies.

Double-check findings
Sampled familiar tool start/JSON/end tokens reached 100% teacher-forced top-1.
Held-out generation still chose invalid operations or the wrong tool.
Both 128- and 512-token original-suite evaluations scored 0/6; two of those
checks were blocked downstream of an earlier context failure.
No evidence currently justifies an optimizer replacement as the first fix.
The 20-session development suite is narrow and is not final usability acceptance.
