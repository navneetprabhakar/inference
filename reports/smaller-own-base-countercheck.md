# Smaller own-base countercheck

Research note, 2026-11-06. This note changes no code, training, budgets, queue, or acceptance criteria. The current randomly initialized 124M foundation and three 20M-target optimizer-screen arms must finish unchanged. Each arm has its own continuation; their token counts are not cumulative training of one model.

## Decision supported by the evidence

Primary research supports learning simple narrative language with much smaller models and deliberately simplified, diverse data. It does **not** establish that a 10–40M model will reach useful conversation plus calculator/unit-tool correctness sooner than continuing our 124M checkpoint at equal local wall time. A smaller restart is a testable candidate, not a demonstrated repair.

Corpus documents, words, unique tokens, processed token positions and loss-bearing targets are different quantities. No word-to-token conversion or epoch count is inferred where sources do not provide the necessary denominator. Reported token positions may include padding or positions excluded from loss; the reproduction card does not resolve that detail. Synthetic data can originate from larger pretrained teachers while the trained model's own weights still start randomly; this differs from training solely on human-authored data.

## Parameter-count correction

The **SimpleStories paper's Table 2** explicitly identifies its TinyStories-33M comparator as approximately **68M total parameters**, and explains that TinyStories model names exclude embeddings while its own names include them. That is a source-attributed count for that comparator, not an independently reconstructed count for every original TinyStories experiment. The original paper mentions retaining only the top 10K most common tokenizer tokens; vocabulary representation, embedding tying, and released configuration therefore matter when reconstructing exact totals. Do not automatically apply the 68M figure to every configuration called “33M.”

Use total trainable parameters when designing a 10–40M candidate. A large vocabulary can dominate tiny models. The SimpleStories result makes vocabulary reduction worth testing, but its domain-specific WordPiece tokenizer has not been shown suitable for our dialogue, numbers, units, and tool schemas.

## Local implication and bounded next decision

- At the observed approximately 1,450 targets/second for the **current** model, 100M targets correspond to about 19.2 hours, 327.68M to 62.8 hours, and 1B to 8 days at that average rate. The observed rate includes this run's evaluation and checkpoint overhead; future overhead and interruptions can differ. These arithmetic conversions are not forecasts for smaller models or direct reproductions of published runs.
- Finish the current optimizer screen unchanged. Preserve its endpoints. It cannot by itself decide model size, tokenizer, or training-data recipe.
- A future 24–40M **total-parameter** candidate is justified as a bounded experiment if a local throughput benchmark shows meaningful savings. Parameter ratios alone do not predict M4 performance; vocabulary projection, kernels, sequence length, and batch shape matter.
- Compare the candidate with continuation of the current 124M endpoint under an equal **additional wall-time** budget and approved training mixture. Count tokenization/setup overhead explicitly. This is a pragmatic next-investment comparison, not a clean causal architecture comparison because the 124M model already has training history.
- Require improvements on development-only short conversation, context tracking, tool selection, argument extraction, and correct use of tool results. Story quality or loss alone cannot justify replacing the current base. With different tokenizers, raw per-token loss is not comparable; use task outcomes and appropriately normalized language measures.
- Favor continuing 124M if relevant held-out metrics keep improving, the small candidate offers little measured speed advantage, or its story gains do not transfer to dialogue/tool semantics. Favor a smaller restart only after measured compute savings translate into better relevant quality within the bounded comparison.

No source reviewed establishes the required mixture, exposure, or success probability for our target. Chinchilla-style compute-optimal ratios are not a universal quality minimum, and TinyStories fluency is not an assistant-usability guarantee.
