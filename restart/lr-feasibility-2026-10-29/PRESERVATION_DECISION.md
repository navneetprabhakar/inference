# Preservation decision for the proposed research screen

Decision fixed before inspecting development performance: **the four-arm screen is an acquisition-versus-native-narrative-retention experiment, not a model promotion or acceptable-conversation test.** Keep the original own foundation as the common source. Do not replace it with an already adapted checkpoint to make the behavior baseline look stronger.

The 0/80 source behavior score makes a five-percentage-point behavior-decline gate vacuous. Remove that gate from this source's research eligibility and retain the packet as acquisition/regression evidence. A floor-aware result remains INCONCLUSIVE, never PASS. Do not treat a future higher behavior score as proof that all original capabilities were preserved.

Use the same frozen 512-block native validation set at the source and scheduled checkpoints. Report NLL, absolute change and percentage change from source, plus acquisition accuracy; compare matched canonical/diverse arms at the same replay coefficient. Native NLL is an explicit proxy for the foundation's trained narrative distribution. It is neither general-English coverage nor a measure of conversational usability. The historical +5% line is displayed only as a reference, not used to stop no-replay research or declare success.

Keep prerequisite acquisition thresholds unchanged (cell D overall >=90%, each task >=80%, whole state conversations >=80%, both-correct counterfactual groups >=80%). Meeting those thresholds means **prerequisite acquisition qualified for further study**, not “preservation passed.” No single acquisition score can compensate silently for narrative degradation.

At the endpoint, retain the full acquisition/NLL tradeoff for all arms. A condition is dominated if another has at least as good cell-D accuracy and no worse native NLL, with one strict improvement. Report dominated arms too. Choose a same-replay canonical/diverse pair for further-seed confirmation using the existing endpoint-only rule; label that choice experimental. If acquisition improves while native NLL worsens, record the tradeoff and require a separate follow-up decision. Do not promote a checkpoint automatically.

This resolves the preservation decision for the research screen without inventing a new numeric tolerance after seeing trained results. A future natural-conversation release still requires a representative behavior benchmark and reviewed preservation evidence appropriate to the actual source. Neither the 64 short cases nor native narrative NLL alone can supply that release gate.

No final-test payloads or development scores are consulted in learning-rate selection. Retained training remains unapproved during this calibration.
